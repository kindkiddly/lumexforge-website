Why a protein breakfast helps

Protein is the most filling macronutrient, so a 25 g breakfast tends to curb mid-morning snacking. It also helps you spread protein evenly through the day, which supports muscle when you train.

If you are vegetarian, dairy (yoghurt, skyr, cottage cheese, milk) and soy (tofu, soy milk) are your strongest egg-free options. Vegans can rely on tofu, soy milk, pea protein, peanut butter and beans.

tips

Make it easy on busy mornings

01

Prep the night before

Overnight oats and yoghurt pots take two minutes the evening before.

02

Choose higher-protein milk

Soy milk has ~8 g per cup; almond and oat milk have 1–3 g.

03

Read yoghurt labels

Plain Greek yoghurt or skyr has about double the protein of regular yoghurt.

04

Add a scoop

Half a scoop of protein powder adds ~12 g to oats or a smoothie.

05

Keep tofu ready

Crumble a block of firm tofu on Sunday and reheat portions all week.

Frequently asked questions

What is a high protein breakfast without eggs?

Greek yoghurt or skyr bowls, cottage cheese on toast, tofu scramble, protein overnight oats, smoked salmon bagels and protein smoothies each provide around 20–30 g of protein without eggs.

What is a high protein vegetarian breakfast?

Dairy and soy are the strongest options: Greek yoghurt, skyr, cottage cheese, milk, tofu and soy milk, plus nuts, seeds and oats.

How much protein should breakfast have?

Around 20–40 g suits most adults, depending on body size and goals.

Is oatmeal high in protein?

Oats alone have about 5–6 g per 50 g. Cooking them in milk or soy milk and adding protein powder or peanut butter can bring a bowl to 20–30 g.
