Is Cal AI accurate?

Cal AI's own FAQ puts its accuracy at around 80%. That's useful, but on a 600-calorie meal it can still mean a gap of over 100 calories.

By the ProteinSnaps team · Updated October 2026

Cal AI is reasonably accurate for simple plates and less so for mixed, oily or sauced dishes, like every photo-based tracker. Treat results as estimates and correct portions.

where it slips

Where photo AI struggles

  • Hidden cooking oil and butter (≈ 120 kcal per tablespoon of oil)
  • Sauces, dressings and gravies
  • Stews, curries and casseroles
  • Portion depth in bowls
  • Drinks and shakes
paywall

Scanning sits behind a subscription

Cal AI requires a paid plan for photo scanning, so most people can't test accuracy on their own food first. If you want to compare, choose an app that lets you scan on a free tier.

tips

How to get closer numbers with any app

  1. Shoot top-down in good light.
  2. Keep foods separated.
  3. Add a note for oil, butter and sauces.
  4. Log packaged foods from the label.
  5. Weigh a few staple portions once to calibrate your eye.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How accurate is Cal AI?

Cal AI's FAQ cites roughly 80% accuracy. Simple plates do better; mixed and oily dishes do worse.

Is Cal AI free?

Photo scanning requires a subscription.

Which is more accurate, photo AI or manual logging?

Weighed manual logging is most accurate; photo AI is far faster and good enough for most goals when you correct obvious errors.
